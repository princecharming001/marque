# Studio standard tier: an edit for under $0.20

_Owner requirement (2026-10-09): total marginal cost per video under $0.20 with a margin, the way the mass-market editors do it. This is the investigated answer and the build plan; nothing here is implemented yet except the no-regret steps noted in HANDOFF.md._


## 1. Why the engine costs $5-36 and the mass-market apps cost cents

**Our cost is the loop, not the judgment.** The Director is an agent that edits the CutDocument through 36 tools over 9 stages in 50-158 model calls, and every call re-reads a 33,457-token fixed prefix (36 tool schemas ~28.7k tokens + the SKILL.md block 4.8k), the doctrine files it loaded (219k chars on the Opus d030 job, 652k on the Fable job), 2-4.4k-token contact sheets (622-701 image exposures per job) and its own hidden thinking replayed as input (35% of a 208k-token peak context). Then the champion loop (full render -> 3-4 critic calls with 5-11 images -> Director revision -> pairwise judges, up to 12 rounds) spent 90% of the bill and won 1 of 10 decided rounds; two rounds were spent on an engine bug since fixed. The bill is 50% cache writes (1h TTL, 2x input), 36% output/thinking, 14% cache reads. Measured: $5.77 (Opus 5.5 Director alone) to $18.39 (the same job after a Fable fallback) and $36.20 (Fable). Repricing the same tokens on Haiku 5.5 still gives $0.40-1.79 per video, so a cheaper model alone cannot reach $0.20. What the Director actually decided on d030 was tiny: two sentence drops, five pins, one 1.15x punch-in, a caption size change and four emphasis words (oplog: 49 edit_caption_page, 13 set_captions, 11 set_framing, 18 notes, 1 set_story, 1 cut_words). Everything else it emitted was driving deterministic tools we already have. On top of that, 2-8 full renders at x264 veryslow cost ~41 CPU-minutes per video.

**They never built the loop.** Every mass-market tool whose docs we could read (Opus Clip, Submagic, Klap, Veed, Vizard, Gling, FireCut, CapCut, Riverside) is the same pipeline: one ASR pass (AssemblyAI $0.15/hr, Deepgram $0.26/hr, Scribe $0.22/hr), code for everything computable from transcript + VAD + face track (silence, fillers, retakes by sentence similarity, caption templates, reframe, zoom heuristics, loudness), 1-3 small text-only JSON calls over the transcript (Opus Clip uses Gemini Flash; the LLM line is under $0.01 per video), one render on fast encoders, and the human as the critic in an editor. Nobody re-renders after an automated critique, nobody puts a frontier model or images in the loop, and nobody scores the rendered file. Their marginal cost is $0.04-0.10 per clip; Submagic's founder reports 80% gross margin on $0.58-0.98 per video, i.e. $0.12-0.20 all-in COGS including hosting. The two products with genuinely agentic editing (Descript Underlord on Claude, Captions chat edits) do not flat-price it: they meter every prompt. So the honest answer to "why can't we": we can, because the expensive half of our engine is optional. The deterministic half (verbatim Take Index with word IDs, retake clusters, gap snap points, compiler, face-aware caption placer, measured voice chain, loudness, 10 invariants) is already better than what they have, and it costs cents. What we give up at this price is the same thing they give up: nobody reviews the finished video before the creator sees it.

## 2. Recommended design: Studio tiers (Auto Edit standard / Director Edit / Pro), with grafts and fixes applied

Standard tier = the deterministic engine unchanged + ONE structured-output planner call + one render + the 10 invariants + code-only repair, shipped marked NOT REVIEWED. The Director/critic loop survives untouched as the Pro tier behind a tier flag. Default planner: `claude-sonnet-5-5` at effort `low` (Haiku 5.5 behind a flag for a free tier; the probe in section 4 decides the default). Prices verified today on platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing: Sonnet 5.5 $2/$10 per MTok (cache read $0.10, footnote 2); Haiku 5.5 $0.10/$0.50 for prompts up to 100k tokens ($0.50/$2.50 above); ElevenLabs Scribe v2 $0.22/hour (elevenlabs.io/pricing/api).

Reading this honestly: the thing the owner asked for (model + ASR + paid APIs) is $0.04-0.05 per video, 4-5x under $0.20, and $0.01 on Haiku. Compute is the line that decides the margin, and it depends on the host and on utilisation: an always-on Render box only reaches these figures at roughly 1,500-2,500 videos/month; at 500/month the $175 instance alone is $0.35 per video, so run renders on per-second scale-to-zero capacity (Fly) or spot/batch, or state the break-even volume. Fixed costs: Render $175/mo; Remotion $0 for a team of up to 3 people, else $100/mo minimum (10,000 renders) at $0.01/render; no ElevenLabs plan needed for API billing. Worst case inside the tier (90 s source, planner retry, one repair re-render, per-minute ASR rounding): API $0.09, all-in ~$0.20 on Render, ~$0.15 on Fly. Hard rules that keep it there: output <=90 s (prefer 75 s on the flat tier), source <=10 min, paid voice isolation ($0.12/min) only when `music_in_room` and the source <=60 s and the running API subtotal stays <=$0.17, otherwise the local Wiener path with "needs isolation add-on" in the report; music/SFX/b-roll never on the standard tier. 4K60 sources: today's full-resolution mezzanine makes a 60 s 4K60 upload ~165-180 vCPU-min ($0.28-0.30 compute on Render) before any API cost, which is why stage 1 downscales to 1080p in the standard tier; after that fix a 4K60 video costs the 1080p figures plus ~$0.01-0.03 for the one-time 4K decode.

### Quality: kept and lost

Kept, unchanged in code and still hard gates on the one shipped render: all 10 invariants (clicks/cut-offs with the ASR round trip, A/V alignment, no model timestamps - the planner only names IDs, pins, licences, HDR once, caption geometry vs the face track, loudness/true peak on the encoded file, no digital silence, delivery format); the verbatim Take Index; retake clusters and the "false start needs a later re-delivery" rule; gap snap points, breaths kept whole, pads that never cross the snap; equal-power crossfades and room tone; the measured voice chain; the face-aware caption placer whose `auto` ladder produced every shipped placement on d030/take40/4k60 (35/36, 33/34, 18/18 pages above_head at one height - the Directors only changed size); a rational timeline snapped once to the frame grid; a report that names every removed range's reason and every seam's measured cause; a normal CutDocument + oplog so `studio chat`, `studio qa` and a Pro upgrade are a resume, not a re-edit. The planner's schema carries every decision the Director actually made on the real jobs (the `Part one.` drop, the payoff pin, the 1.15x lean-in, caption emphasis, take40's `1/3 2/3 3/3` labels, the var-silences "restates the hook" drop).

Lost, against the reviewed agentic edit: no one watches the rendered file, so taste defects (sagging middle, choppy rhythm, swipe moments, a punch-in that reads late, "end on the payoff vs on the instruction") ship unreviewed and the report says NOT REVIEWED rather than implying approval; story judgment is one Sonnet/Haiku call with one retry and no listener re-read of the radio test, so expect some over-kept preamble and the occasional wrong take (mitigated by the CTA guard, the "never drop >50% of words" cap, pins from the same call, and the pure-code fallback); pacing is rule-based (relative to the speaker's median, fillers only in silence or the first sentence) rather than heard; seams carry measured causes, not prose; one house caption style sized by measurement, no hook title; zero punch-ins unless the planner asks and the clean-scale check allows; no b-roll, cards, music, SFX or colour looks (doctrine-valid "none", but a tutorial that needs "show" footage loses that job); no variants, no pairwise, no questions to critics; music-in-room takes longer than 60 s get the local denoise. This is the competitors' product shape, where 20-40% of clips get discarded or re-edited in a timeline; our floor is higher (gated clicks, geometry, loudness, verbatim cuts) and our ceiling is far below the Director's.

## 5. Product recommendation

Price per finished video or per output minute, never per source minute (at OpusClip's $0.10 per source minute a 90 s source leaves ~25-30% margin; per video leaves 65-85%). Three tiers from one codebase: **Auto Edit (standard)**: marginal $0.04 model+ASR, $0.08-0.15 all-in at 1080p30 for 30-60 s outputs, sold inside a $12-19/month plan with a monthly video cap like Submagic/Opus Clip (revenue $0.40-1.00 per video, ~65-85% gross margin after compute, break-even on the fixed Render/Remotion floor at roughly 2,000-2,500 videos/month on one box, or near zero fixed cost on scale-to-zero capacity); include 3-5 chat edits per video and meter the rest at 2 credits (~$0.10) each against a measured $0.03-0.09 marginal, the way Captions.ai charges per message; Haiku-planner free tier with a watermark. **Pro review** add-on: the standard cut plus one Opus 5.5 champion-loop round resumed from the standard document (~$1.50-2.50 measured per round), sold at $5-10 or 100-200 credits, with a cheaper "what a pro would change" notes-only rung (~$0.65 cost, ~$1 retail). **Director's cut**: today's full loop (Opus 5.5/high ~$8.80 all-in; Fable 5.1 $15-36) at $15-25 per video or on the creator's own key (BYOK: we charge compute + ASR only), with `max_model_usd` so it can never run to $36 again, and the Fable model never on house billing. Every tier's report says what was checked ("NOT REVIEWED: standard tier" vs "reviewed by Opus 5.5 + Sonnet 5") so the price and the promise match.

## Appendix A: how this plan was produced (2026-10-09)
Thirteen agents in one workflow: four investigators (trace token profile of the five real jobs; competitor pipelines and pricing; verified price sheets for models, ASR and compute; a codebase map of what is deterministic today and what only the Director decides), three independent designs (deterministic-first, single-shot planner, tiered product), two judges, one adversarial refutation per design, and a synthesis.

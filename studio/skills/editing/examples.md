# Worked examples

Five complete edits, from brief to critique, ordered from the lightest touch to the heaviest. Read them for judgment, not templates: each one shows the cut decisions by quoted line, the finishing choices, and what was deliberately left out. Example 1 is the real calibration take (`backend/eval/pro_cut_reference.py`), where the right edit was nearly untouched. The others are realistic composites. Their measurements are illustrative, so `evidence.md` does not ledger them; every doctrine prior they use comes from the topic files and `constants.yaml`.

---

## 1. The calibration take: one cut, not nineteen

**Take.** A cooking creator, one continuous iPhone take of 50 s, 140 words, medium energy. It opens with three false-started hook attempts and has one mid-sentence stumble. A professional editor cut it, and the old engine cut it; the two cuts were aligned word by word.

> "Most fusion— most fusion—" · 1.14 s gap · "most fusion fails for the same reason." · "You're not matching— you're— most fusion fails for the same reason." · "You're matching flavors instead of fat, acid, and heat. Everyone tries to pair fusion by taste. Gochujang tastes bold, carbonara tastes rich. Throw them together, done." · 577 ms · "That's why it collapses. Flavor isn't the structure. Fat, acid, and heat are." · … · "So I match the axes first. Cut the gochujang with a splash of vinegar to give the fat something to lean on. Pull the heat back so it seasons instead of screams. Then it works. Rate that version" · 690 ms · "7 out of 10. Follow for the next collision test."

**Brief.** One idea: "Fusion fails when you pair by taste; balance fat, acid and heat instead." Audience: home cooks who have tried a mash-up. Payoff: "So I match the axes first … Then it works." Protected: the whole CTA, "Rate that version 7 out of 10. Follow for the next collision test." Style: explainer. Energy: medium, conversational pauses around 350–480 ms.

**Story cut.**
- "Most fusion— most fusion—": cut. Two false starts, each re-delivered later.
- "most fusion fails for the same reason.": kept as the hook. It is the first *complete* delivery; the second complete one is no better, and delivery decides, not order.
- "You're not matching— you're— most fusion fails for the same reason.": cut. This restart is the one mid-video content splice. It rejoins at "You're matching flavors…", a sentence boundary, so it plays as an honest jump between hook and body.
- Everything else stays in the creator's order, including the CTA, which the old planner had once deleted as a "false start".

**Fine cut.**
- A 0.27 s stumble inside "So I match the axes first" is removed.
- The two longest stalls are tightened: 577 ms after "done." (just past the 250–550 ms keep band for a sentence boundary) and 690 ms inside "Rate that version … 7" (a mid-phrase hesitation).
- About 17 of the 22 pauses of 300 ms or more are kept to the millisecond (385 ms after "heat.", 481 ms, 368 ms …). Silence goes from 32% to 30%.
- No speed change: nothing is slow once the stalls are gone.

The old engine cut every gap over 350 ms down to 200 ms: 19 dead-air micro-splices, a breathless, choppy take. The pro's edit is about 42 s long with four small mid-video edits, each removing a named problem. A seams-per-minute critic will ask about ~6/min; the answer is the list of problems, and that ends the question.

**Finishing (the Director's choices; the reference covers only the cut).**
- **Framing:** base 1.0×. If the source is 4K, one 1.3× punch on "So I match the axes first", held to "Then it works." and returning to base on "Rate". It does two jobs: it hides the stumble repair and marks the turn to the payoff at about two-thirds of the runtime. On 1080p, no punch; the repair sits in a still moment.
- **Inserts:** none, unless the creator has a clip of the finished dish. That would be the one insert worth requesting: a face-kept split under "Then it works." as proof. Stock gochujang or carbonara was rejected as noun matching.
- **Captions:** phrase style, mixed case. The first page, "Most fusion fails", is up at frame 0, so no hook title is needed. Accents only where prosody confirms, expected on "structure" and "vinegar": about 3 of 30 pages.
- **Sound:** high-pass, gentle compression, −14 LUFS. No music: dense reasoning at a conversational clip. No SFX.
- **Colour:** measure; no correction if the face is in range.
- **Ending:** out-point 0.3 s after "test." No loop, because "…collision test." does not run into "Most fusion fails…".

**Deliberately not added:** a whoosh or zoom on the splice, zoom pulses on "fat, acid, and heat", stock food b-roll, a bed, a "follow for more" card, any trim inside the CTA, and pause normalisation.

## 2. A three-take explainer that needs more seams

**Take.** "Dev", a tech explainer, medium energy (about 165 wpm), iPhone front camera, 4K60 HLG. Three takes over 2 min 40 s: T1 by a window at 4 pm, T2 and T3 under a desk lamp half an hour later.

> T1 (0:00–0:48): "Okay so, um, I keep getting DMs about this, so— your iPhone storage, when it says full…" … "Mine was forty-one gigs of memes my group chat sent in 2021." (laughs)
> T2 (0:50–1:40): "Your iPhone isn't full of photos. It's full of texts." · "Every video anyone sends you gets saved, forever, and it, like, you know, keeps every— wait, where was I."
> T3 (1:42–2:40): "Messages keeps every photo and video anyone sends you, forever. Here's the fix. Settings, General, iPhone Storage, Messages, Review Large Attachments. Swipe to delete. Follow for part two: the 'System Data' thing."

**Brief.** One idea: "Full iPhone storage is usually old Messages attachments, and one screen deletes them." Audience: people who have already deleted photos and are still full. Payoff: the menu path. Promise: "It's full of texts." Protected: the laugh after "forty-one gigs", and the CTA (part two is confirmed in creator memory). Style: explainer with tutorial steps. Energy: conversational. Length window: 40–60 s.

**Story cut.**
- T1's preamble ("Okay so, um, I keep getting DMs…"): cut.
- Hook: T2's "Your iPhone isn't full of photos. It's full of texts." It passes the checks: clear, specific, a gap, a falling finish, eyes on the lens.
- Proof: T1's "Mine was forty-one gigs of memes my group chat sent in 2021.", with the laugh. It is the only personal proof, and it is funnier than anything in T2 or T3. It is comped in at a sentence boundary.
- Mechanism: T2's "Every video anyone sends you gets saved…" loses its thread ("wait, where was I"), and T3 re-delivers it completely, so T2's attempt is a false start. T3 carries the mechanism, the steps and the CTA.

**Fine cut.**
- Hook → proof (T2 → T1): a take change at a beat boundary. T1's face is 6% smaller, so it is scaled up 1.06× to match within 5%, and the join is left as an honest jump.
- Proof → mechanism (T1 → T3): another beat boundary, another honest jump. Skin is matched in colour (step below).
- Inside T3, one hesitation ("forever, [0.9 s] and") is tightened to 0.35 s. The head barely moves, so the seam is invisible.
- The steps stay at 1.0× with their natural 250–320 ms gaps: instructions need processing time.
- Result: about 52 s with 5 seams, ~5.8/min. That is above the 1–4 prior for a clean take, and correct: every seam removes a retake, a false start or a stall.

**Finishing.**
- **Visual hook:** in T2 the creator holds the phone up to the lens showing the real "iPhone Storage Almost Full" alert. That frame is frame 0.
- **Inserts:** one split screen, the screen recording in the top 45% and the face below with the eyes above the bottom UI band, from "Settings" to "Swipe to delete" (about 6 s), cropped to the menu so its text renders at ~50 px, with one highlight box on "Review Large Attachments". Job: show the steps. It is the creator's own recording.
- **Framing:** no punch-ins. The steps live in the split, and the other seams sit at beat boundaries.
- **Captions:** phrase style. They move to the clear band during the split. "41 GB" is shown as digits and accented; no card, because the caption carries the number.
- **Colour:** T1 is 0.4 EV brighter and cooler. It is matched to T2/T3 on skin (ΔE2000 1.6) and face luma; the window-lit background still differs, which is acceptable at a beat boundary.
- **Sound:** one chain for all three takes, room tone under every seam. No music, no SFX.
- **Ending:** 0.35 s after "thing."

**Deliberately not added:** a hook title (the held-up alert and the first caption page pass the mute test), stock "storage full" graphics, a speed-up of the steps, tap sounds the recording never had, zooms on "forty-one gigs", and a progress bar.

## 3. A founder's bad-news update

**Take.** A fintech founder, calm to medium energy, one continuous 71 s take on the company account. 4K30 SDR.

> "Hey everyone, it's Sam, I run Tallyho." · "Last Tuesday we double-charged two hundred and twelve of you." · "It was a bug in a release we pushed that afternoon. To be fair, our payment processor's retry logic made it worse, but—" · "that's on us." · [0.9 s, eyes on lens] "We got this wrong." · "Every one of you got a refund and an email from me yesterday." · "We've, uh— we've added a second check before any charge runs twice." · "If you were affected and haven't heard from us, reply here and I'll fix it myself."

**Brief.** One idea: "We double-charged 212 customers, we refunded everyone, and here is what changed." Audience: customers and followers. Goal: trust. Protected: "that's on us", the 0.9 s pause and "We got this wrong", the soft CTA. Style: founder/brand, bad news. Energy: calm. Intensity follows the news: the quietest dials.

**Story cut.**
- "Hey everyone, it's Sam, I run Tallyho.": the greeting is cut, but the name and role return as a lower third on the first line, because on a company account who is apologising matters.
- Hook: "Last Tuesday we double-charged two hundred and twelve of you." The stake, first.
- "To be fair, our payment processor's retry logic made it worse, but—": ambiguous. Cutting it makes the apology cleaner; keeping it is the founder's own account of events, and cutting it changes the meaning of what he said. The editor does not decide that alone. Two variants go to the creator: A keeps the clause, B cuts it at the clean gap before "that's on us". B is recommended.
- Everything else stays, in order.

**Fine cut.** Pauses stay at the calm speaker's own 400–600 ms. The 0.9 s pause before "We got this wrong." is kept whole: it is the beat. The flub "We've, uh— we've added" stays: scrubbing it would make the apology sound like an ad read. Zero seams in variant A; one in variant B, at a clause boundary, invisible in a still head.

**Finishing.**
- **Framing:** none.
- **Inserts:** one face-kept split under "Every one of you got a refund and an email from me yesterday": the real refund email with customer names and amounts blurred, about 3 s. Job: proof. Nothing appears over "We got this wrong."
- **Text:** lower third "Sam Ortiz · CEO, Tallyho" (4 words, so a 2.2 s reading minimum; shown 3 s, inside the 3–5 s prior) on the hook line. Captions in the quiet sentence style: mixed case, no accents, no emoji.
- **Sound:** light chain only. No music, no SFX.
- **Colour:** no correction, no look.
- **Ending:** 0.4 s after "myself."

**Deliberately not added:** a "WE MESSED UP" hook title, a sad piano bed, a punch-in on "We got this wrong", stock "apology" imagery, a speed-up, any trim of the 0.9 s pause, and a second ask.

## 4. A high-energy listicle where more finishing is right

**Take.** A fitness creator, high energy (arousal 0.7, about 190 wpm), 4K60 HLG, 64 s raw with a long sign-off and one retaken item.

> "Four things I'd stop doing in the gym if I started over." · "One: ego lifting. Nobody cares what's on the bar. Your joints do." · "Two: skipping legs because they're hard. That's exactly why you do them." · "Three: sooo many people, like, just wander between machines—" (restarts) "Three: no plan. Wandering between machines isn't training." · "Four: training through pain. Sore is fine. Sharp is not." · "Save this for leg day. Okay, that's it, love you guys, bye!"

**Brief.** One idea: "Four habits that waste beginners' first year." Payoff: item four, the safety line. Protected: "Save this for leg day." Style: listicle. Energy: high. Budget: one marker system, a light bed, one sound family.

**Story cut.** The count is spoken first, and the ordinals are spoken, so there is no reorder. The first attempt at item three ("sooo many people, like, just wander…") is cut, because it is re-delivered cleanly. "Okay, that's it, love you guys, bye!" is cut; the CTA stays.

**Fine cut.** Boundary pauses of 200–300 ms. A hard picture cut at every item boundary, picture-only where the speech flows. No speed change: after the retake cut, the slowest item runs at 176 wpm. A global 1.15× was rejected: it would speed the hook and the safety line and stack with viewers' own 2×. Result: 31 s.

**Finishing.**
- **Framing:** alternating 1.0× and 1.25× on each item's first word, identical every time, so it reads as a system. No other punches.
- **Markers:** a "1/4" to "4/4" pill in the title zone, entering on each ordinal, same preset every time.
- **SFX:** one soft wooden tick on each pill's first frame, the same atonal sample, peaking about 6 dB under speech. Nothing on the hook's first word or on "Sharp is not."
- **Music:** a light instrumental bed at ~100 BPM, 17 LU under speech. The hook is voice-only; the bed enters on the downbeat after "over." It drops out for the beat before "Sharp is not." and lands its button on "day." Three marks in total.
- **Insert:** one 1.5 s cutaway of the creator's own clip of a wobbling overloaded squat on "Your joints do." Job: show the claim. No stock gym footage.
- **Captions:** phrase style; caps on 1–2 word pages; the accent colour only on "Sharp".
- **Ending:** 0.3 s after "day." At 31 s it could loop, but "…leg day." does not run into "Four things…", so no loop.

**Deliberately not added:** a whoosh on every cut, zoom pulses, a second punch size, emoji, meme sounds, a speed ramp, a progress bar, and a riser before item four.

## 5. A paid UGC ad with compliance and variants

**Take.** A creator paid by a budgeting app, medium-high energy, 4K30, 46 s raw. The brief is marked paid and supplies the offer code.

> "Hey so this is a paid partnership with Pocketwise." · "Are you broke by the twentieth every single month?" · "I connected my bank, and in like two minutes it showed me a hundred and forty dollars in subscriptions I forgot about." · "It also says it saves the average user six hundred dollars a year." · "Code SAVE20 gets you twenty percent off. Link in bio."

**Brief.** One idea: "Pocketwise found $140 in subscriptions I'd forgotten about." Goal: install. Protected: the spoken disclosure, the code, the price claim's words, the CTA. Style: sales/UGC with compliance floor. Destinations: TikTok, Reels (Business account), Shorts. Length: 20–35 s.

**Compliance pass (before the story cut).**
- The spoken disclosure is the creator's first line. Keep every word of it, and add an "Ad · Paid partnership with Pocketwise" label (5 words, so at least 2.5 s) in the top band from 0.0 s. Flag the platform toggles for the creator.
- "saves the average user six hundred dollars a year" is an objective claim. It stays in the audio with no stat card, and the creator is asked to confirm that Pocketwise can substantiate it.
- "Are you broke by the twentieth…" asserts a personal attribute (financial status), which Meta's ad policy bars. It stays for TikTok and Shorts, and the Meta variant opens on the proof line instead.

**Story cut.** Hook (TikTok/Shorts): "Are you broke by the twentieth every single month?", with the spoken disclosure moved to directly after it; the on-screen label keeps the disclosure upfront from frame 0. Hook (Reels): the proof line, verbatim, "I connected my bank, and in like two minutes it showed me a hundred and forty dollars in subscriptions I forgot about." Result: 27 s. Three more hook alternates are rendered from real lines for the brand's ad group.

**Fine cut.** 200–300 ms boundary pauses; fillers out of the hook; "in like two minutes" keeps its "like" mid-clause. The demo line runs at 1.05×, the hook and CTA at 1.0×.

**Finishing.**
- **Inserts:** the creator's own screen recording in a split (face below) from "connected my bank" to "forgot about", with the $140 subscriptions list cropped to ~50 px text and one highlight box. A second product moment: a 1.5 s pickup of the app's summary screen on "Pocketwise". The real product appears twice. No stock, no AI.
- **Framing:** one 1.3× punch on "a hundred and forty dollars". The CTA stays at base framing.
- **Captions:** phrase style, "Pocketwise" spelled from the brief, "$140" in digits. The code card "SAVE20 · 20% off" runs from "Code" to the end.
- **Sound:** a commercially cleared bed 18 LU under speech, lifting into the CTA; one soft pop on the code card; a no-music master.
- **Deliverables:** the TikTok/Shorts master, the Reels variant, three extra hook alternates, a textless export for the brand's overlays, and a no-music master.

**Deliberately not added:** a "$600/year" stat card, stock "stressed person with bills" footage, a logo on frame 0, an AI "customer", a trending song, whooshes on cuts, and a speed-up of the hook.

## What the five have in common

- Every seam removes a named problem; seam counts are explained, never targeted.
- Protected lines (hooks, confessions, safety lines, CTAs) stay at 1.0× with nothing stacked on them.
- Each insert is the creator's own footage or a real capture, with a named job; stock appears in none of the five.
- Music appears only where it has a job (list momentum, an ad), and a no-music master always ships.
- When a decision changes meaning, the creator gets variants instead of the editor's guess.
